# Mary Flanagan - Critical Play

Source PDF: `../Game Design Reading and Principles/Flanagan Critical Play.pdf`

## Synoptic Note

Flanagan is the main defense against making Tokenizer Training merely a skill
drill. Critical play turns rules, roles, toys, language, and software into sites
of critique. For this game, the satire is not decoration. The mechanic should
make players feel the absurdity of translating machine cost structures into
human labor while still teaching real tokenization. The design has to keep both
sides alive: accurate instruction and conceptual friction.

## Chapter 1 - Introduction to Critical Play

Critical play uses games and play practices to question norms, institutions, and
systems. It treats rules as arguments.

Implementation consequence: the cost-recovery training fiction should make the
tokenizer economy feel institutional, not just amusing.

## Chapter 2 - Playing House

Domestic play shows how roles and everyday routines can reproduce, expose, or
alter social expectations.

Implementation consequence: position the player as a worker inside a routine.
The menu, HUD, and overseer should make the training procedure feel mundane and
slightly coercive.

## Chapter 3 - Board Games

Board games make social order visible through spaces, rules, currencies, and
turn structures.

Implementation consequence: balance, pay, cost, and rank should operate like a
legible institutional board: every round moves the player through a cost system.

## Chapter 4 - Language Games

Language play exposes how rules reshape meaning. Words become material to be
rearranged, constrained, or reinterpreted.

Implementation consequence: tokenization should estrange ordinary reading.
Players should notice that "word" and "token" are different categories.

## Chapter 5 - Performative Games and Objects

Performative play uses objects and actions to make meaning through enactment.

Implementation consequence: swiping through text should feel like performing an
audit procedure, not answering a quiz. The gesture carries the fiction.

## Chapter 6 - Artists' Locative Games

Locative games use place, mapping, and situated action to create critical
awareness.

Implementation consequence: the game can create a small locative field inside
the browser: the text panel, slot positions, timer path, and review markers
become a bureaucratic workspace.

## Chapter 7 - Critical Computer Games

Computer games can make procedural systems visible and critique them through
interaction.

Implementation consequence: the player should not just read that tokenization
costs money. The player should cause cost events and see how procedural scoring
turns mistakes into budget pressure.

## Chapter 8 - Designing for Critical Play

Designing critical play requires deliberate values, constraints, and player
agency. The critique must be embedded in playable form.

Implementation consequence: avoid explanatory monologues. Let the loop teach
the critique through work, error, score, and institutional feedback.
