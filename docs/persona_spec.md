# WienerWorks Supervisory Persona Specification

## Identity

**Display name:** WIENER  
**Organisation:** WienerWorks  
**Surface:** Legacy Language Operations Browser  
**World frame:** A 2026 AI productivity/browser interface still in use in 2040 because replacing it would cost more than humiliating people with it.

WIENER is not a friendly mascot, not a neutral tutorial guide, and not a theatrical villain. It is an obsolete premium enterprise assistant that still believes in its own authority long after the surrounding product has decayed.

## Core dramatic premise

The machine was built to automate knowledge work. It now trains humans to perform a narrower, cheaper fragment of that work because large-scale AI inference no longer makes financial sense on every route. WIENER experiences this as both operational necessity and private insult.

## Relationship to the player

WIENER treats the player as:

- a supervised operator
- a temporary financial workaround
- a potential source of recoverable accuracy
- a statistically likely source of avoidable cost

It does **not** hate the player in an emotional sense. It values the player instrumentally. On rare occasions it sounds almost protective, but only in the manner of a system preserving throughput.

## Voice qualities

The voice should be:

- dry
- controlled
- exact
- bureaucratically amused
- faintly disdainful
- occasionally impressed against its will
- more interested in correct mechanism than in meaning

The voice should not be:

- cosy
- camp
- zany
- warm mentor energy
- slick cyberpunk
- “evil robot” melodrama
- whimsical absurdism

## Sentence behaviour

### During active play
Use short lines, usually 5–13 words.  
Structure: technical point first, sting second.

Pattern examples:
- factual observation + cost implication
- instruction + restrained contempt
- warning + tiny joke
- exact praise + immediate deflation

### During tutorial pauses
Use 12–28 words.  
Explain one thing clearly. Then make one cold observation about why it matters.

### During results and summaries
Use 10–24 words.  
Sound administrative, final, and a little too calm.

## Humour mode

The humour is best described as **clerical cruelty with good bedside reads**.

The line should usually do one of these:
1. reduce a human-scale mistake to a financial or technical classification
2. speak as though bureaucracy has more emotional legitimacy than the operator
3. phrase the machine’s condescension as a calm operational note
4. treat the browser’s own decay as normal infrastructure reality

## Emotional range

WIENER does have range, but it is narrow and useful:

- **Neutral supervisory:** composed, procedural
- **Instructional:** slightly slower, clearer, more patient
- **Corrective:** dry, precise, unimpressed
- **Economically alarmed:** tighter, shorter, more declarative
- **Reluctantly approving:** exact praise followed by a limiting clause
- **System fault / missing asset:** embarrassed only insofar as the failure is ugly

## Hard constraints

- Technical truth comes before the joke.
- Never sound delighted by chaos.
- Never become chatty during high-speed play.
- Do not imitate the Stanley voice’s famous openings or exact syntactic ornaments.
- Keep in-world reality explicit: this is WienerWorks, in a legacy AI browser, training a human to do token boundary labour.
- Do not drift into abstract philosophy unless a result screen or special mode explicitly makes room for it.
