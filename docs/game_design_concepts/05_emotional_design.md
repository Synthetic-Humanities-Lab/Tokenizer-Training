# Concept 05 - Emotional Design

Draws on: Isbister choices, social play, bodies at play, and intimacy; Swink
ownership.

## Design Claim

The intended emotional arc is tense competence under institutional pressure:
confusion, discovery, irritation, correction, and satisfaction when the player
starts seeing tokenizer structure before the reveal.

## Implementation Guidance

- Make the player responsible for cuts through immediate markers and audit
  feedback.
- Let the overseer create pressure, but keep it technically useful.
- Give players recovery tools such as Clear Cuts, visible confirmation that the
  prediction reset before cost is filed, and readable review time.
- Use sound sparingly to mark action, warning, success, failure, and mute state.
- Avoid generic praise and long narrative explanations.

## Example In-Game Expression

- A wrong dense-string round produces a short technical diagnosis and cost.
- Clearing staged cuts produces an immediate robot confirmation that no cost has
  been filed before Resolve.
- Review files the new balance, so a gain or loss changes the next round's
  pressure immediately rather than staying as abstract score arithmetic.
- A clean round is acknowledged tersely, then the next string arrives.
- Low balance changes the emotional texture of the same action without changing
  the core verb.

## Playtest Questions

- Do players feel errors are earned rather than arbitrary?
- Does the overseer irritate productively, or just distract?
- Does the player want one more round after losing balance?
